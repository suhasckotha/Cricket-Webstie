Basic Rules of Cricket

The Core Objective

Cricket takes place on a large oval field with a 22-yard strip in the center called the pitch. Two teams of 11 players take turns batting (trying to score runs) and fielding/bowling (trying to limit runs and take wickets). The team with the highest run total at the end wins.

How Scoring Works

Physical Runs: After hitting the ball, two batters run back and forth between the wickets at opposite ends of the pitch. Each successful run equals 1 point. Boundary Four (4 Runs): If a hit ball bounces before crossing the outer field boundary line, the batting team automatically gets 4 runs. Boundary Six (6 Runs): If a hit ball crosses the boundary line completely in the air without touching the ground, the batting team gets 6 runs.

Ways a Batter Gets "Out" (Dismissed)

  • Bowled: The bowler throws the ball, misses the bat, and hits the wooden wickets behind the batter.
  • Caught: A fielder catches the hit ball in the air before it touches the ground.
  • Run Out: Fielders hit the wooden wickets with the ball while the batter is outside their safe area (crease).
  • Leg Before Wicket (LBW): The ball hits the batter's leg/body in line with the wicket when it would have otherwise hit the stumps.
